**Short answer:** AI agents are worth it for a trade or field service business only if they do one of three things better than you do now: answer the after-hours call, get the quote out before the customer forgets they asked, or send the van to the right job without three phone calls first. The four jobs worth automating, in order of value, are after-hours enquiries, turning job notes into quotes, scheduling and dispatch, and chasing routine admin. Start with one, not all four.

Search "AI agents for trades and field service businesses" and you will mostly find software vendors describing features, not businesses describing what changed. That gap matters, because a plumber, electrician, or landscaper does not need "AI" - they need the after-hours call answered, the quote out before the customer forgets they asked, and the van sent to the right job without three phone calls first. AI agents for trades and field service businesses are worth looking at only if they do one of those three things better than what you are doing now.

ABS figures show AI adoption among Australian businesses jumped from around 1% in 2021-22 to roughly 12% in 2024-25 - a fast climb, but construction and trade services still sit behind professional and technical services, which lead the pack. That lag is not stubbornness. It is that most of what gets marketed to trades is generic office software repackaged with an AI label, not something built around how a field business actually runs its week.

## Why aren't businesses in Australia using AI agents?

The barriers named in every survey are time, knowledge and messy data rather than technology. For a trade business, that narrows the case to the admin side - the after-hours call, the quote, the schedule - where the work repeats and the information already exists.

**What that means for a trade or field service business:**

- **Adoption is rising but shallow.** It is 12% on the strict count, 44-72% on any-use surveys, and under 10% significant. Most have tried something; few have changed how they operate.
- **Trades trail every ranking, and non-adopters mostly cite irrelevance or distrust.** That is a gap of examples, not attitude.
- **The trade is safe; the front office is not.** Reception, bookkeeping and quoting are where AI lands first, which is exactly where a field business loses its evenings.
- **The barriers are time, knowledge and data.** If job history, pricing and customer details live in three places, no agent can fix that.

## What does an AI agent actually do for a trade business?

Strip away the marketing and there are four jobs worth automating, in roughly this order of value:

- **Answering after-hours enquiries.** A call or web enquiry outside business hours gets captured straight away - name, number, what they need - with a reply confirming someone will call back, instead of going to voicemail. For an emergency trade, this is usually the highest-value fix, because it is the one most likely to be losing you a job tonight. Industry estimates put the missed-call rate for Australian trade businesses at roughly one in three, and Harvard Business Review's audit of 2,241 companies found that firms replying within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to win the lead as those that waited even an hour longer.
- **Turning job notes into a quote.** The agent pulls your standard rates, the technician's notes from a similar past job, and a template into a draft quote ready for you to check and send, instead of you rebuilding it from scratch at 9pm. Australian tradies spend an estimated 8 to 12 hours a week on admin, with quoting and invoicing the biggest share - a full day a week off the tools.
- **Scheduling and dispatch.** New jobs get slotted against who is free, where they already are, and what the job needs - fewer calls to work out who can get there, and automatic reminders so fewer customers forget a booking. The reminder half of this is well proven: a systematic review of 29 studies found automated reminders cut no-shows by an average of 28.9%.
- **Chasing the boring admin.** Review requests after a job is marked complete, and polite follow-ups on overdue invoices, sent on a schedule instead of when you remember. Xero's data on 520,000 Australian small businesses shows invoices paid an average of 6.6 days late, and a 2025 GoCardless survey found over a quarter of SMBs spend up to an hour a week chasing them.

None of these need a technician-facing robot or a chatbot pretending to be a person. The best version of each is invisible to the customer and just makes the paperwork move faster.

## What does this look like in practice?

The same four jobs turn up well beyond the trades. Four recent examples from our client work at Grid Concepts:

- **A real estate agent.** "After-hours enquiries go to whoever is on call." Sunday-night portal enquiries sat until Monday, by which time serious buyers had booked inspections elsewhere. Now every enquiry is captured and confirmed the minute it arrives, and lands in the CRM.
- **A construction company.** "Our estimator loses a day a week rebuilding quotes." The costings for near-identical jobs already existed in old spreadsheets. Draft quotes are now assembled from the closest past job and the current rate card; the estimator checks instead of types.
- **A property lawyer.** "Clients call at 7pm when the contract lands, and we are closed." Before any tool, we settled which system would see client details and how that squares with the Privacy Act. The capture takes only name, number and matter type - nothing from the contract - so the firm gained response time without a privacy problem.
- **A salesperson.** "I am on the road four days a week, so quotes wait until Friday." The price list lived in his head. It moved into a product sheet, and quotes are now drafted from the note he dictates after each visit, with the follow-up scheduled automatically.

## Why does the same AI agent work for one trade business and not the next?

The tool is rarely the reason an AI agent underperforms. It is that the job history lives in one app, the customer's phone number lives in a spreadsheet, and the pricing lives in someone's head - so an agent built to speed up quoting has nothing reliable to pull from. We cover this gap in more detail in [Fix the data first - then AI works](/insights/fix-the-data-first/), and go deeper on quoting, scheduling and after-hours specifically in [Automating quoting, scheduling and after-hours enquiries for service businesses](/insights/automating-quoting-scheduling-after-hours-enquiries/).

Field service adds one more wrinkle professional-services agents do not have to deal with: the agent needs to know where your team is and what job they are already on, not just what is in a database. If your scheduling still lives on a whiteboard or in three people's heads, that is the thing to fix before an AI agent goes anywhere near it.

## Where should a trade business start with AI agents?

Pick the single task above that is costing you the most right now - for most emergency and callout trades, that is missed after-hours enquiries, because you can count them before and after. Do not try to fix all four at once, and do not sign up for a system built for accounting firms and relabelled for trades.

## Frequently asked questions

### What can an AI agent do for a plumber, electrician or landscaper?

It handles admin, not the trade itself: capturing after-hours enquiries, drafting quotes from past jobs, slotting jobs into the schedule with reminders, and chasing reviews and overdue invoices.

### What is the best first AI task for a trade business?

For most emergency and callout trades, answering after-hours enquiries, because missed calls are the most likely cause of a lost job tonight and you can count them before and after.

### How many Australian trade businesses use AI?

ABS figures show about 12% of Australian businesses used AI in 2024-25. The National AI Centre puts SME adoption at 44% on an any-use count, but under 30% in construction and agriculture.

### Why do AI agents fail in field service businesses?

Usually because the data underneath is scattered: job history in one app, phone numbers in a spreadsheet, pricing in someone's head. Field service also needs the agent to know where the team is and what job they are on.

### Does a tradie need a chatbot that pretends to be a person?

No. The best versions are invisible to the customer and simply make the paperwork move faster.
