A customer fills in your website form at 9pm, gets an automatic "thanks, we'll be in touch," and hears nothing for two days. By the time someone calls back, they have already booked with whoever answered first. This is what slow lead response time looks like from the customer's side, and it is quietly costing service businesses jobs they never find out they lost - because a cold enquiry does not complain, it just goes quiet.

## What "going cold" actually costs

The research on this is blunt. A widely cited analysis of leads contacted within five minutes versus 30 minutes found a 21-times difference in qualification rates, and a separate large-scale study of first-touch response found that leads called back within 60 seconds converted at roughly four times the rate of leads called back after 30 minutes. On the Australian side, a survey of leading Australian organisations found 34% did not respond to an email enquiry at all, and only 21% of those that did reply managed it within 24 hours. A separate industry audit found replies ranging from 25 minutes to never. None of these businesses think of themselves as slow - they just have no system that flags an enquiry the moment it lands, so it waits behind whatever job is in front of the owner that day.

## Why the delay happens

It is rarely a lack of effort. It is almost always one of three gaps:

- **Nobody is watching the inbox after hours.** A form submission at 7pm sits until someone opens their laptop the next morning, by which point the customer has already called two competitors.
- **The enquiry lands in the wrong place.** A website form that emails one inbox, a Facebook message in another app, and a phone call that goes to voicemail all need separate checking - so something always gets missed.
- **Following up takes more than a glance.** If replying means finding the enquiry, checking whether you can even take the job, and writing a reply from scratch, it gets pushed to "later," and later becomes two days.

## Fixing it without overbuilding

Fixing slow lead response time does not require a full CRM overhaul or a chatbot that tries to sound human. It requires three much smaller things, in order:

**First, get every enquiry into one place.** Website forms, email, and any other channel you actually use should land somewhere you (or someone) checks constantly, not spread across three inboxes and an app you forget exists.

**Second, send an instant acknowledgement.** Not a sales pitch - just confirmation that the enquiry arrived and a real person will follow up, ideally with a rough timeframe. This alone stops a lot of the "did this even send?" drop-off.

**Third, flag anything urgent so a human follows up fast.** Most enquiries can wait a few hours. A few cannot, and if your system cannot tell the difference, everything gets treated like it can wait - which is how the five-minute window turns into two days.

## Why this breaks down without the data behind it

A fast-reply tool bolted onto messy records will still lose jobs - it will just lose them faster. If your customer and job details live in three places and nobody is sure which is current, an instant reply to the wrong contact, or a "yes we can do Tuesday" that clashes with a job already booked, causes its own kind of damage.
